# CS-360-Mobile-Architect---Programming

|Module quetions|My response| 
|-----:|-----------|
|1. Briefly summarize the requirements and goals of the app you developed. What user needs was this app designed to address? | Event Tracking, was built to help users stay organized by managing important events and receiving timely SMS reminders so they never miss birthdays, meetings, or special occasions. Its core requirements include adding, editing, and deleting events; persisting data across app restarts; and offering a secure login to protect personal information. The app was designed to address the need for a simple, reliable event‑reminders tool that fits into busy schedules while minimizing friction and avoiding unnecessary permissions or features.|
|2. What screens and features were necessary to support user needs and produce a user-centered UI for the app? How did your UI designs keep users in mind? Why were your designs successful? | The app uses several key screen login, event list, add‑event dialog, edit‑event dialog, and settings to support user needs in a straightforward, user‑centered way. Essential features include one‑tap event CRUD, SMS reminder toggles, and a clean, icon‑free list layout that keeps the focus on dates and descriptions. The UI stays user‑centered by limiting clutter, using clear labels, grouping actions logically, and sizing tap targets appropriately, which makes the app accessible on both small and large screens and explains why users can quickly understand and complete tasks without help.|
|3. How did you approach the process of coding your app? What techniques or strategies did you use? How could those techniques or strategies be applied in the future? | I approached coding by first sketching the app’s architecture separating data storage (SharedPreferences with Gson), UI controllers (Activities/Fragments), and business logic (event management and SMS setup) then building incrementally and integrating layers one at a time. I used modular, reusable components, meaningful naming, and consistent lifecycle patterns (for example, letting sensors or listeners start in onResume and stop in onPause) so the code would stay readable and testable. These techniques can be applied in future projects by supporting easier debugging, refactoring, and team collaboration, especially when working with more complex stacks or larger codebases.|
|4. How did you test to ensure your code was functional? Why is this process important, and what did it reveal? | Testing involved running the app on emulators and physical devices, walking through typical user flows (add/edit/delete an event, toggle SMS on/off, log in and out), and verifying that data persisted correctly after closing and reopening the app. I also used Logcat and breakpoints to trace sensor data, permission grants, and lifecycle events, which revealed issues such as incorrect listener registration, timing‑related bugs, or data‑save failures that could otherwise go unnoticed. This testing process is important because it catches regressions early, ensures the app behaves consistently, and builds confidence that the delivered product actually meets user expectations.|
|5. Consider the full app design and development process from initial planning to finalization. Where did you have to innovate to overcome a challenge? | Across the full design and development process, innovation was needed particularly in balancing sensor or background activity efficiency with battery life and simplifying the SMS reminder flow so it felt optional and user‑controlled. For example, instead of always listening to sensors or polling events, I adopted lifecycle‑aware patterns and only started listeners when the app is visible, and I structured SMS enablement behind a clear settings screen and optional first‑time prompt. This kind of design thinking prioritizes user privacy, battery friendliness, and progressive disclosure can be reused in future apps by turning complex system level features into intuitive, low‑friction experiences.|
|6. In what specific component of your mobile app were you particularly successful in demonstrating your knowledge, skills, and experience? | The most successful component in demonstrating my knowledge and skills was the end‑to‑end integration of event management with SMS reminders, including secure login, persistent storage, and proper permission handling. This part showed proficiency with Android fundamentals Activities, intents, SharedPreferences/Gson, SMS permissions, and dialogs while also reflecting attention to usability, performance, and privacy. By shipping a coherent feature flow that users can understand and rely on, this component clearly illustrates both technical depth and a user‑centered engineering mindset.|
